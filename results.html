The timeline

From pre-launch strategy in 2022 to a university-wide center in 2026.

  1. 2022

    Pre-launch strategy

    • Strategy discussions about a university venture fund, a student founder community, and tech transfer, before any program existed.
    • The sequence decided here (programs and pipeline first, fund alongside, policy in parallel) shaped everything after.
  2. 2023

    Center launched

    • Center for innovation and entrepreneurship launched inside the business school.
    • Venture fund partnership agreement signed with an outside manager, creating a university-affiliated fund.
    • First bank-sponsored pitch competition with an annual prize pool.
    • First youth entrepreneurship summer camp.
    • First cohort of a women founders accelerator built with a community partner.
  3. 2024

    Recognition and curriculum

    • National award for an emerging entrepreneurship center.
    • Second accelerator cohort.
    • New courses: introduction to entrepreneurship, introduction to venture capital, and a global innovation course abroad.
    • First national rankings survey submission.
  4. 2025

    Donor-funded programs and a national ranking

    • Microgrant program and pre-accelerator funded by donor gifts.
    • Princeton Review and Entrepreneur Top 50 undergraduate entrepreneurship program.
    • Seven-figure naming gift for student space and scholarships.
  5. 2026

    University-wide

    • Center elevated to a university-wide center.
    • High school innovation challenge expanded after a pilot with more than 140 students.
    • Third accelerator cohort.
    • VERIFY: rank #42 for 2026 on the Princeton Review and Entrepreneur list.
    • VERIFY: 2026 Model Emerging Program Award from a national association.

Programs built

We design each of these program types from proprietary playbooks: rules, rubrics, budgets, and calendars, adapted to your institution.

Student founder fellowship and community

A cohort-based fellowship and a standing community for student founders, with requirements, tracking, and a named student space funded by a seven-figure gift.

Microgrants

Small grants for interdisciplinary faculty and student projects at the earliest technology readiness levels, with written guidelines and a review process.

Pre-accelerator

Run with a national partner and funded by donor gifts, feeding the pitch competition and the fund.

Pitch competition

Bank-sponsored, with an annual prize pool, published rules, eligibility, a judging rubric, and a sponsor package.

Women founders accelerator

Built with a community partner, now in its third cohort.

High school innovation challenge

A regional pipeline program that piloted with more than 140 students and expanded in 2026.

Youth entrepreneurship summer camp

An annual camp with its own playbook and budget.

Curriculum

Introductory entrepreneurship and venture capital courses, a global innovation course abroad, and courses built on MIT Disciplined Entrepreneurship.

University-affiliated venture fund

Built through a partnership agreement with an outside manager, from structure to deployment.

The venture fund model

The fund was built from the fund structure and the university partnership agreement through fundraising and capital deployment. The structure is the part that can transfer to your institution.

Carried interest to the university

A share of the fund's carried interest returns to the university, so the institution participates in the upside without managing the money.

Joint investment committee

University and manager representatives sit together on the investment committee, which gives the institution a voice without making it the manager.

Student sub-fund

A student-managed sub-fund inside the structure, which makes the fund a teaching vehicle and a pipeline.

Outside manager

An independent manager raises, invests, and reports. The university does not hold securities licenses or run a fund operation.

What we learned

Lessons from the build, written as advice you can apply.

  1. 01

    Start with programs that create a pipeline before you start a fund.

    A fund needs ventures to invest in. The fellowship, the competition, and the microgrants came first and ran alongside the fund partnership so there was dealflow from the start.

  2. 02

    Launch where you can, and design for the whole institution.

    The center started in the business school because that is where it could start. The charter, the governance, and the programs were written from day one for a university-wide mandate, which made the later elevation a formality.

  3. 03

    Secure the sponsor before you announce the prize.

    Sponsorship packages with named levels and specific benefits turn a competition into a renewable revenue line. The prize pool was sponsored, not budgeted.

  4. 04

    Build the rankings data system in year one.

    The first national rankings submission was possible because course counts, program numbers, and venture outcomes had been tracked since launch. Rankings reward institutions that can answer the questions.

  5. 05

    Name things donors can give to.

    Student space, a fellowship, a microgrant pool, and a pre-accelerator each had a name and a number before anyone was asked. The naming gift and the donor-funded programs followed from a gift menu, not a general appeal.

  6. 06

    Partner for reach you cannot build.

    The women founders accelerator and the pre-accelerator each ran with a partner who brought reach and credibility the center could not have built alone in three years.

  7. 07

    Let the fund be someone else's job.

    The university participates in the economics and the governance of the fund. An outside manager raises and invests. That separation kept the center from becoming a fund operation and let the fund move at a manager's pace.
