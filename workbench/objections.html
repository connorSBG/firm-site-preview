Objections and draft answers

14 objections we expect to hear, with the answer we would give today. Edit src/content/objections.json.

  1. 01

    “Our board has already decided we are starting a fund.”

    Then you need the partnership terms, the governance, the investment policy, and the pipeline to make it work, and you need them before counsel starts drafting. We have built that structure. We will also tell you, in the decision memo, what has to be true for the fund to succeed, so the board goes in with eyes open.

    capital
  2. 02

    “We already have a consultant.”

    Good. What did they hand you? If it was a report with recommendations, we start where it stopped: the operating documents, the playbooks, the policies, the budget, and the capital structure. If they are an operator running a program for you, we can help you decide what to own and what to keep renting.

    alternatives
  3. 03

    “Can't we just copy what TU did?”

    You can start from it, which is what our library is. But the sequence, the budget, the donor case, and the capital pathway depend on your research base, your alumni, your region, and your leadership. The Diagnostic exists to work out what transfers and what does not. Copying the surface without the sequence is how programs stall.

    credibility
  4. 04

    “We can figure this out ourselves.”

    Many institutions do, over three to five years, by reinventing the same documents and making the same mistakes. What we sell is the sequence and the templates, so your three years become one. If you have the time and the person, you may not need us. Most do not have the person.

    alternatives
  5. 05

    “We could hire someone for that.”

    You could, and you probably will. The question is what you hire them to do. A qualified leader costs roughly $250K to $300K a year all in. Hiring before the charter, the program portfolio, the budget, and the capital decision exist means paying that salary while they figure out what you should have told them. We write the plan and the job description. Then you hire the right person into a job that already makes sense.

    pricealternatives
  6. 06

    “Why not just join a network?”

    Join one. Networks give you peers, content, and dealflow. They do not write your charter, your IP policy, your donor case, or your fund terms, and the capital-gated ones require commitments most mid-sized institutions cannot justify. We build the thing the network assumes you already have.

    alternatives
  7. 07

    “We have no budget.”

    Most of our clients did not either, at first. Planning work is a common use of a foundation gift, a trustee gift, a state commercialization grant, or a dean's discretionary fund. A $10,000 Diagnostic sits under most signature thresholds and is credited toward later work. We can help you frame the ask to a donor. We will not write the grant.

    price
  8. 08

    “Why would we take fund advice from people who do not manage our money?”

    Because that is the only advice worth taking. Anyone who will manage the fund has an interest in you starting one. We have no stake in the answer, so we can tell you whether you need a fund, a microgrant pool, a student fund, a sidecar, or nothing yet. When you do need a manager, we help you pick one and set the terms.

    capital
  9. 09

    “You are in Oklahoma and we are not.”

    Almost all of the work is remote. We travel for kickoff, working sessions, and the readout. The programs, policies, and capital structures we design are not regional. The regional part, your donors and your partners, is exactly what the Diagnostic maps.

    logistics
  10. 10

    “Our procurement process will take months.”

    The Diagnostic is priced to fit under most direct-purchase thresholds, and it is a fixed-fee, fixed-scope agreement with no hourly billing to review. We have worked with university and health system procurement before and can supply what they need. Start the Diagnostic while the larger engagement goes through the process.

    priceprocess
  11. 11

    “You have one case study.”

    We have one institution where we did all of it, from launch to a national ranking to an affiliated fund, in about three years. That is the point. We are selling the sequence we ran, not a survey of what others did. The template library has been used in real programs with real donors and students.

    credibility
  12. 12

    “Rankings are a game. We do not want to play it.”

    Rankings are a data system with a narrative on top. The data system (what you count, who counts it, when) is the same one your board and your donors need. Build it for them, and the rankings submission is a byproduct. We are not interested in gaming anything. We are interested in you being able to answer the questions.

    credibility
  13. 13

    “We need someone to run it.”

    Then you need a hire or an operator, and we can tell you which. We design so your hire has a plan on day one. If you have nobody at all, the Build tier includes the staffing plan and job descriptions, and Blueprint + Coach includes a year of coaching for whoever you bring in. We will not be your interim director.

    scope
  14. 14

    “You are too small.”

    Two senior practitioners who have built these programs is the right size for design work. You get the people who did it, not analysts. We keep scope tight and we turn down work that needs a bigger firm. The alternative is paying a large firm for a team that has read about it.

    credibility
